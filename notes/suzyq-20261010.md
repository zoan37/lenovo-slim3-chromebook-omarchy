# SuzyQ debug cable, CCD open (2026-10-10)

A home-made SuzyQ (closed-case debugging cable) gives the Chromebook's consoles and a remote hard reset to another
machine over USB, with nothing opened up: the GSC (Ti50, Google's security chip) exposes them on the SBU pins of
one USB-C port.

## The cable

USB-C male breakout board (24-pin, screw terminals) to a USB-A male terminal plug:

| USB-C plug | to |
|---|---|
| A5 CC1 | 22 kΩ to VBUS (A4) |
| B5 CC2 (labelled VCONN on a plug board) | 56 kΩ to VBUS (B4) |
| A8 SBU1 | USB-A D+ |
| B8 SBU2 | USB-A D- |
| VBUS (A9) | USB-A VBUS |
| GND (B12) | USB-A GND |

The USB-C end goes into the **Chromebook**, the USB-A end into the host (here a Dell XPS 13, through an A-to-C
adapter). The two different pull-ups to VBUS are what the GSC detects as a debug accessory (and which way round the
plug is); the Chromebook also sinks 5 V from the host through them.

**It answers on one port and one orientation only: USB-C port 0 (typec `port0`, EC `p0`), plug reversed
(`orientation=reverse`).** In the other three combinations the Chromebook sees an ordinary 5 V source
(`TypeCSnkAttached` in `/sys/kernel/debug/cros_ec/pdinfo`) and the host sees nothing. Plugged in the wrong way round
(C end in the host) both ends source VBUS into each other: don't.

On the host: `18d1:5066 Google Inc. Ti50` and four serial ports, named by their USB interface strings:

| | interface | |
|---|---|---|
| ttyUSB0 | Shell | the GSC's own console (`ccd`, `version`, `wp`, `ecrst`) |
| ttyUSB1 | AP | the SoC's UART0 (`/dev/ttyS0` on the Chromebook) |
| ttyUSB2 | EC | the embedded controller (power states, lid, battery, PD, its own log buffer) |
| ttyUSB3 | FPMCU | the fingerprint MCU slot (no sensor on this model) |

115200 8N1; the host user needs the `uucp` group. [`scripts/suzyq.sh`](../scripts/suzyq.sh) lists them, logs one,
sends a command, or hard-resets the Chromebook (`ecrst pulse`).

## Kernel console on the AP UART

The mainline cmdline starts with `console=ttyS0,115200n8 console=tty0` ([`mainline-pack.sh`](../scripts/mainline-pack.sh)):
the whole kernel log and systemd's serial getty (`quigon login:`) show up on the host. tty0 stays `/dev/console`
(listed last). A hang before Wi-Fi, or during suspend/resume, now leaves its last lines on the host even when
pstore has nothing (a power-button reset clears the RAM pstore lives in).

The EC console keeps a log too. Its buffer after the PCIe L1.2 freeze showed the EC's `watchdog interrupt`, then
`chipset_reset` and S0→S3→S5: the AP had stopped answering.

## CCD open

Out of the box CCD is `Locked` (no password): the AP/EC consoles are readable and `FlashRead` works, but typing into
the EC, `RebootECAP`, flashing and `OverrideWP` need it `Opened`, and `OpenFromUSB` is off, so the open has to come
from the Chromebook through the GSC's TPM interface:

1. **TPM on mainline**: [`mt8189-quigon-tpm.dtsi`](../port/quigon/mainline/mt8189-quigon-tpm.dtsi), the Ti50 on I2C3
   0x50 with its interrupt on GPIO 138 (from ChromeOS's DT), driver `tpm_tis_i2c_cr50`, gives `/dev/tpm0`. In the
   default kernel since `def2`. `cros-run gsctool -a -f` (ChromeOS's gsctool from ROOT-A) reports the GSC versions.
2. `cros-run gsctool -a -o`, then short presses of the side power button whenever the GSC asks, for about 5 minutes
   (the first window is a few seconds: start pressing before starting gsctool, or retry it in a loop). Omarchy's
   `HandlePowerKey=ignore` keeps the presses from doing anything else; holding the button would force a power-off.
   The GSC console shows each press (`RBOX: power button pressed`, `PP press counted!`), then `CCD Opened`.
   **Opening clears the TPM** (`OpenNoTPMWipe` is IfOpened): ChromeOS's encrypted user data on KERN-A/ROOT-A starts
   over at its next boot. Omarchy doesn't use the TPM.
3. `ccd reset factory` on the GSC console: every capability `Always` (flags 0x00400004), so it stays open across
   reboots and battery cut-offs, and hardware write protect is off (`RBOX: WP false`). No firmware was written.

Now: EC console commands work (`version`: Nuvoton NPCX9, RW `obiwan-16378.292.0`), and `suzyq.sh reset` can restart
a hung Chromebook remotely.

## Tests with the SuzyQ attached (2026-10-10)

- **Cold boots:** 10 hard resets (`suzyq.sh reset`, `ecrst pulse`) in a row: every one came up on the default
  mainline kernel, SSH after ~26 s, the desktop and all 4 audio outputs up, ext4 replaying its journal once (expected
  after a power cut), `quigon-doctor` clean.
- **Kernel panic:** `echo c > /proc/sysrq-trigger` (SysRq enabled for that boot only): `panic=10` rebooted it on its
  own, back on the default kernel ~28 s later, and the panic was in pstore (`dmesg-ramoops-0`).
- **Firmware backup over CCD:** `sudo flashrom -p raiden_debug_spi:target=AP -r <file>` on the host (the GSC holds
  the AP in reset during the read; it boots normally after). The 8 MiB image matches the 2026-10-09 backup except
  in the regions the firmware writes itself (`RW_ELOG` 3024 bytes, `RW_MRC_CACHE` 725, `RW_NVRAM` 13); RO, RW and
  the GBB (flags 0x9) are identical. Kept in `private/firmware/`. Mainline has no MTD for the AP flash, so this is
  the way to read it while running mainline.
- Not done yet: a long (hours) sleep with the EC console logging.
